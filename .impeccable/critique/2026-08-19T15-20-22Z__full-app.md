## Design Specificity Verdict

**STRONG.** The "Identity Scanner" creative direction is consistently executed: dark canvas, neon accents, gamified HP-bar progress, sparkle feedback, layer transitions that feel like unlocking game stages. The RIASEC radar with program overlay, campus video carousel, and archetype system are all product-specific. One exception: the lead form is fairly generic — a standard 3-field form that could belong to any lead-capture flow.

## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely well-crafted gamified experience. The test wizard is the standout — the HP-bar progress, layer transitions, and sparkle feedback create an emotional arc that most quiz apps never achieve. The landing hero showing the real product (a live QuestionCard) is a brilliant persuasion mechanism. The biggest opportunity: the results page dilutes its own emotional peak by dumping everything at once instead of orchestrating the reveal.

## What's Working

1. **Hero = Real Product pattern** (Hero.tsx lines 117-138) — Embedding an actual QuestionCard with the real first question transforms the demo into a commitment device. The "Tocá una opción para probar cómo se siente" micro-copy is excellent.

2. **GamifiedProgress bar** (GamifiedProgress.tsx) — 25-segment HP bar grouped by layer (12/5/5/3) with segment-pulse animation on layer completion. Transform-only, respects reduced motion, provides continuous visibility.

3. **Layer transitions as emotional punctuation** (LayerTransition.tsx) — Deliberate pauses between layers prevent the 25-question test from feeling monotonous. The `dismissedTransitions` Set prevents re-showing on back-navigation — a detail most implementations miss.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Auto-playing audio with no upfront consent
**What**: `startTestAudio()` fires inside `handleNext` when the user clicks "Entendido, empezar" on the disclaimer. The disclaimer says nothing about audio. The ~8MB MP3 begins at 15% volume with no opt-in.
**Why it matters**: Violates user control (heuristic #3), wastes mobile data, creates a jarring surprise for a 16-18 audience in quiet environments.
**Fix**: Either add "Reproduciremos música de fondo (opcional)" to the disclaimer with a toggle, or make audio opt-in via the existing toggle button (default off).
**Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden` — add consent framing and default-off behavior.

### [P1] Results page has no progressive disclosure
**What**: ResultsPage renders 8+ content blocks in a single scroll: hero result, archetype card, radar, modality, top 3 programs (expanded), gap analysis, share card, full ranking (all programs), CTAs, disclaimer. No collapse/expand, no "show more."
**Why it matters**: The emotional peak (archetype reveal) is immediately followed by data-heavy sections that dilute the moment. On mobile, this is a very long scroll after a 25-question test.
**Fix**: Collapse the full ranking behind "Ver ranking completo." Move the share card closer to the archetype reveal.
**Suggested command**: `/impeccable distill` — strip to orchestrated reveal, collapse full ranking.

### [P1] Lead form blocks results without framing the value exchange
**What**: After 25 questions, the user hits "Casi listo — Déjanos tus datos para recibir tu resultado personalizado." But the result already exists in sessionStorage. The framing implies the result hasn't been generated.
**Why it matters**: Emotional contract broken: "I did the work, show me the reward" becomes "give me your data first." The "Omitir por ahora →" escape is styled as de-emphasized text — dark-pattern territory for 16-18.
**Fix**: Show a preview (archetype name + emoji) before the form, or reframe: "Guardá tu resultado para no perderlo."
**Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify` — reframe the value exchange.

### [P2] "RIASEC" jargon in layer names without explanation
**What**: Layer 1 is "Intereses RIASEC" — the 16-18 audience likely doesn't know what RIASEC is. The term appears in GamifiedProgress, ArchetypeCard, and RadarChart.
**Why it matters**: Unnecessary cognitive friction for the target audience.
**Fix**: Either explain briefly in the first transition ("un modelo que clasifica tus intereses en 6 dimensiones") or rename to "Tus Intereses."
**Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify` — simplify layer names.

### [P2] Consent text is overwhelming and small
**What**: The consent checkbox label is a dense legal block (Ley 1581 de 2012, NIT numbers, email) rendered at `text-xs`.
**Why it matters**: Unreadable and anxiety-inducing for 16-18 audience.
**Fix**: Summarize in plain language at normal size with a "Ver detalles" expand link.
**Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify` — simplify consent copy.

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (Power User)**: No keyboard shortcuts for navigating 25 questions. Full ranking dumps all programs without sortable columns or filters. No "skip to results" for returning users.

**Jordan (First-Timer, 16-18)**: Disclaimer is the first thing after clicking "Empezar" — lists what the test is NOT, adding pressure. Auto-playing audio could startle in a quiet room. Lead form asks for phone number with dense legal consent — privacy concern for minors.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile, one hand)**: SessionStorage loss on tab switch kills 5 minutes of answers with no partial recovery. Background carousel videos could cause layout shifts on slow connections. Back button is small on mobile left thumb zone.

## Minor Observations

## Questions to Consider

1. Should the lead form appear before or after a results preview? Showing the archetype name + emoji before the form could increase conversion AND reduce abandonment.
2. Is the disclaimer's "what this is NOT" framing right for 16-18? Could it be reframed positively?
3. Should the full ranking be collapsed by default? The top 3 programs get the most attention anyway.
4. Is sessionStorage the right persistence for a 5-minute test that asks for personal data? Losing results = losing leads.
5. Should the audio feature exist at all? Is the immersion worth the data cost and privacy concern?
6. The share card has 3 layout options — is this choice worth the cognitive cost for 16-18 users?
